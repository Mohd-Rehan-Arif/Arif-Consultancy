Overview

What is GST?

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a comprehensive, destination-based indirect tax on the supply of goods and services in India. It came into effect on 1 July 2017, replacing a complex multi-layered system of central and state taxes including Central Excise Duty, Service Tax, VAT, and others.

GST is governed primarily by the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (CGST Act), the Integrated Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (IGST Act), and corresponding State GST Acts. The GST Council, a constitutional body comprising the Finance Ministers of the Union and States, makes recommendations on GST rates, exemptions, and procedural matters.

GST operates on a dual structure: CGST (Central GST) is levied by the Central Government, SGST (State GST) is levied by State Governments on intra-state supplies, and IGST (Integrated GST) is levied on inter-state supplies and imports.

GST applies at multiple rates: 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%, depending on the category of goods or services. A cess is also levied on certain goods above the 28% rate. The applicable rate for any specific supply must be verified from current GST rate schedules and official notifications.

Registration

GST Registration — General Information

GST registration is mandatory for certain categories of persons. Whether registration is required depends on the nature of the business, aggregate turnover, and the type of supply being made.

Threshold Turnover (General)

As a general reference, the aggregate turnover threshold for mandatory GST registration has been set at ₹40 lakh for suppliers of goods (₹20 lakh for special category states) and ₹20 lakh for suppliers of services (₹10 lakh for special category states) in recent years. These thresholds are subject to change by notification. Always verify the current applicable threshold from the official GSTN portal.

Mandatory Registration (Irrespective of Turnover)

  • Persons making inter-state taxable supply of goods
  • Casual taxable persons making taxable supply
  • Non-resident taxable persons
  • Persons required to pay tax under reverse charge mechanism
  • Input service distributors
  • E-commerce operators and certain persons supplying through e-commerce operators
  • Persons required to deduct TDS under GST

General Registration Process

GST registration is completed online through the GSTN portal (gst.gov.in). A GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number) — a 15-digit alphanumeric number — is issued upon successful verification of the application.

Turnover thresholds and registration rules are subject to change by GST Council notification. Always confirm current requirements from the official GSTN portal or a qualified tax professional.

Commonly Required Documents for GST Registration

PAN Card
PAN (Permanent Account Number) of the applicant business entity or proprietor is required for GST registration. For companies and LLPs, the PAN of the entity is used.
Aadhaar Card
Aadhaar card of the proprietor, partners, or directors is generally required. Aadhaar authentication is part of the registration process for most applicants.
Address Proof of Business Premises
Documentary proof of the principal place of business — such as a rent agreement or no-objection certificate (for rented premises), or ownership documents for owned premises.
Bank Account Details
Cancelled cheque or bank account statement showing the account number, IFSC code and account holder name for the business.
Constitution Documents (Companies, LLPs, Partnerships)
For companies: Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association. For LLPs: LLP Agreement and Certificate of Incorporation. For partnerships: Partnership deed. For proprietorships: Identity and address proof of the proprietor.

Document requirements may vary. Confirm current requirements from the GSTN portal or official CBIC guidance.

GST Return Filing — General Information

GST registered taxpayers are required to file periodic returns summarising their outward supplies, inward supplies, tax liability and input tax credit. The type of return and frequency depends on the taxpayer's registration type and turnover.

Input Tax Credit

Input Tax Credit (ITC) — Basic Principles

Input Tax Credit (ITC) is a mechanism under GST that allows a registered taxpayer to reduce the GST paid on purchases (inputs) from the GST payable on sales (output tax). ITC is available for GST paid on goods and services used in the course or furtherance of business.

General Conditions for Availing ITC

  • The taxpayer must possess a valid tax invoice or debit note from a GST-registered supplier.
  • The goods or services must have been received.
  • The tax charged on the supply must have been actually paid to the government.
  • The taxpayer must have filed the relevant GST return.
  • The ITC must be reflected in GSTR-2B (auto-drafted statement).

Blocked ITC (Section 17(5) of CGST Act)

Certain categories of goods and services are specifically blocked from ITC credit under Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017. These include (subject to specified exceptions):

Motor Vehicles and Conveyances
ITC on motor vehicles is generally blocked except where used for transportation of passengers (where business involves such transportation), for further supply of such vehicles, for imparting training on driving/flying/navigating, or for transportation of goods.
Food, Beverages, Club Memberships & Related Services
ITC is generally not available on food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery, club memberships, health and fitness centre services — except where the same category of supply is made or where used for specific exempt purposes.
Works Contract Services (used in Immovable Property)
ITC on works contract services used for construction of immovable property (other than plant and machinery) is generally blocked, even if the immovable property is used for business purposes.
Goods/Services for Personal Use
ITC is not available on goods or services acquired for personal consumption. Only goods and services used in the course or furtherance of business are eligible for ITC.

ITC rules are complex and depend on specific facts. The above is a general educational summary only. Verify ITC eligibility with current law and official guidance.

Notices

GST Notices — General Information

GST authorities may issue various notices for scrutiny, assessment, or recovery purposes. Some commonly encountered notice types:

  • ASMT-10 — Notice for scrutiny of returns, issued when discrepancies are found between filed returns and other information available with the department.
  • DRC-01 / DRC-01A — Summary of demand and intimation of tax, interest and penalty determined. DRC-01A is used for a pre-notice communication before issuing DRC-01.
  • SCN (Show Cause Notice) — Issued before levying demand, penalty, or cancelling registration. The taxpayer is required to show cause why action should not be taken.
  • ASMT-14 — Notice issued for best judgment assessment under Section 62 of the CGST Act, where a taxpayer fails to file returns.

General Guidance on Receiving a GST Notice

  • Do not ignore any GST notice. Ignoring a notice may lead to adverse consequences including ex-parte assessments or penalties.
  • Read the notice carefully to understand its basis, the relevant section of law, and the response deadline.
  • Gather relevant invoices, return filings, and other documents referenced in the notice.
  • Respond within the prescribed time limit. Time limit extensions, where available, should be applied for before the deadline.

This is general educational information about the nature of GST notices. The appropriate response to any specific notice depends on its exact content, the applicable facts, and current law. Seeking qualified professional assistance when dealing with GST notices is advisable.

Compliance

GST Compliance & Recordkeeping

Books of Accounts

GST registered persons are required to maintain accounts and records as prescribed under Section 35 of the CGST Act and related rules. These generally include:

  • Production or manufacture records
  • Inward and outward supply records (purchase and sales registers)
  • Stock records
  • Input tax credit records
  • Output tax payable and paid records

Record Retention Period

GST records must generally be retained for five years from the due date for filing of annual return for the year to which the records pertain (recent amendments should be verified). Records may be maintained electronically.

E-Invoicing

E-invoicing (electronic invoice reporting to the IRP — Invoice Registration Portal) is mandatory for businesses with aggregate turnover above specified thresholds. The current threshold should be verified from the GSTN portal or official CBIC notifications, as it has been revised progressively.
