Income Tax & ITR — General Information & Educational Guide

A general educational overview of income tax law and income tax return (ITR) filing in India, for informational purposes only.

Important Disclaimer This page contains general educational information about income tax law and ITR filing procedure in India. Tax laws, rates, and forms change with each Union Budget and CBDT notification. Always verify current rules from the official Income Tax Department website and the e-filing portal. Nothing on this page constitutes professional legal or tax advice for your specific situation.

Educational Overview

Income Tax in India — An Overview

Income tax is a direct tax levied by the Central Government of India on the income earned by individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), companies, firms, and other entities during a financial year. It is governed primarily by the Income Tax Act, 1961 and the Income Tax Rules, 1962, along with annual Finance Acts that introduce amendments typically through the Union Budget.

Financial Year (FY) and Assessment Year (AY)

Two important concepts in income tax are the Financial Year (FY) and the Assessment Year (AY):

  • Financial Year (FY): The year in which income is earned — runs from 1 April to 31 March. For example, FY 2023-24 runs from 1 April 2023 to 31 March 2024.
  • Assessment Year (AY): The year immediately following the financial year, in which the income of the previous year is assessed (reviewed and taxed). For FY 2023-24, the AY is 2024-25.

When filing an Income Tax Return, taxpayers file for the assessment year corresponding to the financial year in which their income was earned.

Heads of Income

Under the Income Tax Act, income is classified into five heads for the purpose of computation:

  • Salaries: Income from employer-employee relationship, including wages, pension, allowances, and perquisites
  • Income from House Property: Rental income from property owned by the taxpayer (annual lettable value concept applies)
  • Profits and Gains from Business or Profession: Income from carrying on a trade, commerce, manufacture, or profession
  • Capital Gains: Profits arising from transfer of capital assets (short-term and long-term, based on holding period)
  • Income from Other Sources: Residual head covering interest income, dividends, lottery winnings, family pension, etc.

Tax Regimes — Old and New

India currently operates with two tax regimes for individual taxpayers:

  • Old (Existing) Tax Regime: Higher tax rates with the ability to claim various deductions and exemptions (Section 80C, HRA, LTA, etc.)
  • New Tax Regime: Lower tax rates but with limited deductions and exemptions available

The choice of regime significantly impacts the tax liability of individual taxpayers. Tax slabs change with each Union Budget. For current and accurate tax slab information, always refer to the Income Tax Department's official website or the most recent Finance Act.

Return Forms

Types of Income Tax Return (ITR) Forms

The Income Tax Department prescribes different ITR forms for different categories of taxpayers based on their income profile. Filing the correct form is essential. The following is a general reference — form applicability may change each assessment year.

ITR Form Generally Applicable To
ITR-1 (Sahaj) Resident individuals with income from salary/pension, one house property, family pension, and/or other sources (interest, etc.); total income up to ₹50 lakh; not applicable if agricultural income exceeds ₹5,000 or taxpayer has foreign assets/income
ITR-2 Individuals and HUFs not having income from business or profession; applicable for taxpayers with capital gains, multiple house properties, foreign income/assets, or total income exceeding ₹50 lakh
ITR-3 Individuals and HUFs having income from proprietary business or profession (including those with capital gains and other incomes)
ITR-4 (Sugam) Resident individuals, HUFs, and firms (other than LLP) opting for the presumptive taxation scheme under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE; total income up to ₹50 lakh
ITR-5 Firms, LLPs, Association of Persons (AOPs), Body of Individuals (BOIs), Artificial Juridical Persons, Cooperative Societies, Local Authorities (excluding entities filing ITR-7)
ITR-6 Companies (other than companies claiming exemption under Section 11 — income from property held for charitable or religious purposes)
ITR-7 Persons including companies required to furnish returns under Sections 139(4A), 139(4B), 139(4C), or 139(4D) — typically trusts, political parties, research institutions, universities, etc.
Important: ITR form applicability depends on a taxpayer's specific income profile, residential status, and other factors for the relevant assessment year. Forms and their applicability conditions are notified by CBDT each year. Always confirm which form applies to your situation from the e-filing portal or with a qualified tax professional.

Filing Process

General ITR Filing Process

Income Tax Returns are filed online through the Income Tax e-filing portal (www.incometax.gov.in). The following is a general step-by-step overview of the ITR filing process for most individual taxpayers. Actual steps may vary based on the taxpayer's income profile and the applicable ITR form.

1

Collect Income and Deduction Documents

Gather all relevant documents for the financial year: Form 16 from employer(s), salary slips, bank statements, interest certificates, Form 26AS, AIS (Annual Information Statement), TIS (Taxpayer Information Summary), investment proofs, rent receipts, and any other income-related documents.

2

Determine the Applicable ITR Form

Based on the nature and sources of income, residential status, and total income, identify the correct ITR form for the assessment year. Refer to the table above and CBDT notifications for the relevant year's form descriptions.

3

Compute Taxable Income

Calculate gross income under each applicable head, subtract allowable deductions and exemptions (depending on the chosen regime), arrive at net taxable income, and compute the tax liability including applicable surcharge and cess. Verify TDS already deducted and advance tax paid.

4

Verify Form 26AS, AIS and TIS

Form 26AS is a consolidated annual tax statement reflecting TDS, advance tax, and self-assessment tax paid. The Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) contain broader financial information sourced from multiple reporting entities. Reconciling these with your own records is important before filing.

5

File Return on the e-Filing Portal

Login to the Income Tax e-filing portal (www.incometax.gov.in), select the applicable ITR form, fill in all required details, upload any required documents, and submit the return. Ensure all calculations are correct before submission.

6

Verify the Return (e-Verify or ITR-V)

Filing is incomplete without verification. e-Verification can be done using Aadhaar OTP, net banking, bank ATM, or Digital Signature Certificate (DSC). Alternatively, a signed physical ITR-V acknowledgement can be sent by ordinary post to CPC Bengaluru within the prescribed time. An unverified ITR is treated as not filed.

Key ITR Filing Deadlines (General Reference)

Individuals / HUFs / non-audit cases Generally 31 July of the Assessment Year
Audit cases (businesses requiring tax audit under Sec 44AB) Generally 31 October of the Assessment Year
Transfer pricing cases (international / specified domestic transactions) Generally 30 November of the Assessment Year
Belated return (Sec 139(4)) / Updated return (Sec 139(8A)) Subject to applicable statutory provisions

⚠ Due dates are subject to extension by CBDT notification. Always confirm current deadlines from the e-filing portal.

Common Documents Typically Required for ITR Filing

The documents needed depend on the type of income and the applicable ITR form. The following is a general reference list.

Identity & Basic Documents
PAN (Permanent Account Number) — mandatory for all taxpayers. Aadhaar number — mandatory for resident individuals (required for e-verification). Bank account details including IFSC code (for refund credit, if any). PAN-Aadhaar linking should be verified to be in place.
Salary Income Documents
Form 16 issued by employer (Part A: TDS certificate; Part B: breakup of salary and exemptions). Salary slips for the full financial year. Any other employer-issued income certificates. If employed with multiple employers during the year, Form 16 from each employer is required.
Form 26AS, AIS and TIS
Form 26AS is available for download from the e-filing portal and reflects TDS deducted by various deductors, advance tax paid, and self-assessment tax. The Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) provide comprehensive data including interest income, dividend income, securities transactions, and other reportable financial information reported by various entities.
Investment & Deduction Proofs
Proofs for deductions claimed under Chapter VI-A: Section 80C — PPF passbook, LIC premium receipts, ELSS statements, EPF contribution, NSC certificates, home loan principal repayment certificate; Section 80D — health insurance premium receipts; Section 80G — donation receipts; HRA — rent receipts and landlord's PAN (if rent exceeds specified limit); LTA — travel expense proofs as required by employer.
Bank Statements & Interest Certificates
Bank account statements for all accounts held during the financial year. Interest certificates from banks for savings account interest and fixed deposit interest (required for computing taxable interest income). Details of any TDS deducted by bank on interest (reflected in Form 26AS).
Capital Gains & Other Income Documents
Capital gains statements from stockbroker(s) or depository for equity/mutual fund transactions. Sale deed and purchase documents for property transactions (with dates and amounts). Details of any other capital asset transfers during the year. Dividend statements for the year. For foreign income or assets: documents establishing foreign income and assets held.

Notices

Income Tax Notices — General Information

The Income Tax Department may issue various types of notices to taxpayers. Each notice type has a different purpose, legal basis, and prescribed response procedure. The following is a general educational description of some common notice types under the Income Tax Act, 1961.

Section 139(9)

Defective Return Notice

Issued when the Assessing Officer considers the return filed to be defective (e.g., incomplete information, incorrect ITR form used, required schedules not filled). The taxpayer is typically given 15 days (extendable) to rectify the defect. Failure to rectify may result in the return being treated as invalid.

Section 143(1)

Intimation / Summary Processing

An automated intimation issued after the return is processed at the Centralised Processing Centre (CPC). It may reflect a refund, a demand, or no tax effect. If a demand is raised, it should be carefully reviewed and responded to within the specified time. If the intimation is incorrect, a rectification under Section 154 can be filed.

Section 143(2)

Scrutiny Notice

Issued when the Assessing Officer proposes to scrutinise the return in detail. Taxpayers are required to appear before the AO (or respond through the e-proceedings portal) and provide documents, books of account, and explanations. A scrutiny notice must be issued within the prescribed time limit. Do not ignore this notice.

Section 148 / 148A

Reassessment Notice

Issued when the Assessing Officer has reason to believe that income has escaped assessment. Section 148A requires the AO to conduct an inquiry and provide the taxpayer an opportunity to be heard before issuing the Section 148 notice. There are specific time limits for issuing reassessment notices under the law.

Section 156

Demand Notice

Issued when a tax demand is raised following an assessment. The taxpayer is required to pay the demand within 30 days of receipt of the notice. If the taxpayer disputes the demand, an appeal can be filed before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals). Pending appeal, a stay of demand may be applied for.

Section 245

Refund Adjustment Notice

Issued when the Department proposes to adjust an outstanding tax demand against a refund due to the taxpayer. The taxpayer is given an opportunity to respond before the adjustment is made. If the outstanding demand is disputed, the taxpayer should respond accordingly.

General Guidance on Notices: Do not ignore any income tax notice. Read carefully to understand the nature of the notice, identify the response deadline, and gather all relevant supporting documents. Qualified professional assistance is strongly advisable, particularly for scrutiny notices, reassessment notices, and demand notices, to protect your statutory rights and ensure appropriate representation.

Rectification & Appeals

Rectification, Revision & Appeals — Basic Overview

The Income Tax Act provides several mechanisms for taxpayers to seek correction of errors, revise returns, and challenge assessments or orders they consider incorrect. The following is a general educational description of these remedies.

Section 154 — Rectification of Mistakes Apparent from Record

Section 154 allows the Income Tax authority to correct a mistake apparent from the record in any order passed by it. Taxpayers can also file a rectification application under Section 154 to request correction of arithmetical mistakes, clerical errors, or errors that are evident from the records — for example, a TDS credit not reflected in the intimation under Section 143(1). Rectification applications can generally be filed online through the e-filing portal.

Section 139(5) — Revised Return

If a taxpayer discovers any omission or wrong statement in a return filed under Section 139(1) (original return), a revised return can be filed under Section 139(5) before the expiry of three months prior to the end of the relevant assessment year or before the completion of assessment, whichever is earlier. The revised return completely replaces the original return. Only returns filed under Section 139(1) can be revised.

Section 139(8A) — Updated Return (ITR-U)

The Finance Act 2022 introduced the Updated Return (ITR-U) under Section 139(8A), allowing taxpayers to update their returns to include any income not previously disclosed, within two years from the end of the relevant assessment year. Filing an updated return requires payment of additional tax (varying percentage above normal tax/interest). ITR-U cannot be filed to reduce the tax liability, claim refunds, or enhance losses previously reported.

Appeal before CIT(Appeals) — Section 246A

A taxpayer aggrieved by an assessment order, penalty order, or certain other orders of the Assessing Officer can file an appeal before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) [CIT(A)] under Section 246A. An appeal must generally be filed within 30 days of the date of service of the order appealed against (or the notice of demand, in case of tax demand). The appeal should be filed in the prescribed form with the required fee and accompanied by relevant grounds of appeal.

Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT)

The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) is the second appellate authority, which hears appeals against orders of the CIT(Appeals). ITAT orders on questions of fact are generally final; appeals to the High Court (under Section 260A) lie only on substantial questions of law. ITAT proceedings are quasi-judicial in nature.

These are general descriptions of the procedural framework. Timelines, fees, filing requirements, and procedural specifics are prescribed by statute and rules and are subject to change. Professional guidance is advisable for any formal appeal or rectification proceeding.

Deductions & Compliance

Common Deductions & Compliance Notes

Important Note on Deductions: Many common deductions (like Section 80C) are available under the Old Tax Regime but not under the New Tax Regime. Deduction limits, eligibility conditions, and availability are subject to change by Finance Acts. Always verify applicable deductions for the relevant assessment year from the Income Tax Department website or with a qualified professional.

Section 80C — Investment Deductions

Deduction for investments/payments in specified instruments: Life Insurance premium, PPF, EPF, ELSS, NSC, 5-year bank FD, home loan principal, tuition fees, Sukanya Samriddhi. Subject to an overall limit per year (verify current limit).

Section 80D — Health Insurance

Deduction for health insurance premiums paid for self, spouse, children, and parents. Higher deductions available for senior citizens. Includes preventive health check-up amounts within overall limit. Subject to current prescribed limits.

Section 80G — Donations

Deduction for donations made to approved charitable institutions and funds. The deduction percentage (50% or 100%) and applicability of qualifying limit depends on the specific fund/institution to which the donation is made. Donations above ₹2,000 must be made through banking channels.

HRA Exemption

House Rent Allowance (HRA) received from employer can be partially or fully exempt from tax under Section 10(13A). The exempt amount is the minimum of: actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of salary, or 50%/40% of salary (metro/non-metro). Available only under the Old Tax Regime.

Advance Tax

Advance tax is tax paid during the financial year in which income is earned, rather than at the time of filing the return. If total tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year (after accounting for TDS), the taxpayer is required to pay advance tax in instalments as prescribed (typically June, September, December, and March). Non-payment or shortfall in advance tax attracts interest under Sections 234B and 234C. Senior citizens with no business income are exempt from advance tax requirements.

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)

TDS is a mechanism where the payer of certain incomes (salary, interest, rent, professional fees, etc.) deducts tax at the prescribed rate before making payment. The deducted tax is deposited with the government and reflected in the recipient's Form 26AS. The recipient can claim credit for TDS deducted while computing their income tax liability and may receive a refund if TDS exceeds actual tax due. Verifying TDS credits in Form 26AS is an important step in ITR preparation.
